# Zapisy na gry — a guide for players

This page is a sign-up board for board game meetups. It shows who is bringing
what, when it starts and at which table — and you can add yourself with one
click.

This guide explains it all, step by step. You do not need to be good with
computers. Nothing here can be broken.

## 1. What you are looking at

The page shows one meetup. A meetup is divided into **tables**, and each table
has **games** planned on it, one after another.

For every game you will see:

- the **title** and a picture of the box,
- the **start time** and roughly how long it runs,
- **how many people** can play and how many have signed up,
- **who is bringing** it,
- whether that person will **teach the rules**,
- the **list of players** — the names of everyone who has taken a seat.

If the meetup runs over several days, there are tabs at the top for switching
between them.

You do not need to log in to look at any of this. Just open the page.

---

## 2. Signing up for a game

The simplest thing on this page.

1. Find a game you want to play.
2. Click **Sign up**.
3. Type your name.
4. Say whether you know the rules — there is "I know them", "I know them
   somewhat" and "I do not know them".
5. Confirm.

Your name appears on the list for that game. That is all there is to it.

> **Why do we ask about the rules?** So the person bringing the game knows how
> much time to allow for teaching. Nobody is judging you — "I do not know them"
> is a perfectly normal answer, and often the most common one.

If the club asks for it, there will also be a box for your **email address**.
More about that in [section 9](#9-why-give-an-email-address).

---

## 3. The reserve list

If all the seats are taken you can still click sign up — you will join the
**reserve list**.

This is not a dead end. If somebody cancels, the first person on the reserve
list moves up **automatically**. Nobody has to remember to do it or rearrange
anything.

Reserves appear under the list of players, in their own section.

---

## 4. Cancelling

Plans change — that is normal, and cancelling early is a kindness to whoever is
on the reserve list.

There is a **Resign** button next to your name on the list. Click it and you are
out.

If you were logged in, the site already knows it is you. If you signed up as a
guest, it may ask you to confirm — see
[section 9](#9-why-give-an-email-address).

---

## 5. Adding a game of your own

Bringing something along? Add it, and other people can sign up for it.

1. At the table you want, click **Add a game to this table**.
2. Type the title into the search box and pick the game from the list — the
   cover, playing time and complexity fill themselves in.
3. Set the **start time** and the **number of players**.
4. Say whether you will **teach the rules**.
5. Give your name (and an email address if you like —
   [section 9](#9-why-give-an-email-address)).
6. Confirm.

The game appears at the table, and from then on others can sign up for it.

**Cannot find the game in the search?** You can add it by hand — you type the
title and the rest of the details yourself. Useful for homemade games,
prototypes, or very new releases.

**If the club has its own game collection**, you will also see a list of the
games it keeps on site. You pick one from the list and type nothing — and you
do not have to bring anything from home.

## 6. What BGG is

**BGG** is short for **BoardGameGeek** — an enormous online database of board
games. Practically every published game is in it: cover, player count, playing
time, complexity.

For you it means one thing: **you do not have to type any of that in**. You
type the title, click the right entry in the list, and the details arrive by
themselves.

You do not need an account there. You do not even need to know the site exists,
beyond it being the name on a button.

> Sometimes you have to choose between several similar entries: a game may have
> had a few editions, or expansions with similar titles. Pick the one that looks
> right — and if you get it wrong, the entry can always be corrected.

## 7. Adding a new table

If every table is busy but you still have room in the venue, you can add
another: the **Add a new table** button is below the existing tables.

A new table starts empty — add a game to it straight afterwards
([section 5](#5-adding-a-game-of-your-own)).

Some clubs turn this off and let only the organiser add tables. If you do not
see the button, that is why.

## 8. Polls — when nobody knows what to play yet

Sometimes you know that somebody will be playing at that table at 18:00 — but
not what. That is what **polls** are for.

**How it works:**

1. Somebody creates a poll instead of a specific game, using **Add a poll**.
2. They add a few suggestions to it.
3. Everyone else **votes** for what they want to play. Voting also signs you up
   for that game.
4. Once one suggestion has a full set of players, the **poll turns into an
   ordinary game by itself** — and the people who voted for it are already
   signed up.

You can vote for more than one suggestion if several would suit you.

If the person who created the poll allowed it, anyone can add a suggestion of
their own.

> **A poll has a deadline.** If nothing has won by then, the poll can finish on
> its own, and the best-supported suggestion wins. The person who created it can
> also end it early.

## 9. Why give an email address

Giving an address is usually optional, but it buys you two specific things.

**First — you can change your entry later.** Without an account and without an
email address, the site has no way to tell that a game was added by you. With an
address it has: it will ask for that same address, or send a short code to it.

**Second — you get notifications.** See
[section 11](#11-email-notifications).

**Will anyone see my address?** No. Addresses are never shown anywhere. Other
people can send you a message through the site, but they do not see where it
went.

---

## 10. Accounts — why bother

An account is not required. Without one you can still do everything: browse,
sign up, add games.

But an account is more convenient:

- **Your entries are yours.** Nobody else can cancel your seat or delete
  something you added — and you can change your own with one click, without
  confirming anything.
- **You do not type your name every time** — it fills itself in.
- **You get your own game library** ([section 12](#12-your-game-library)).
- **You control your own notifications** ([section 11](#11-email-notifications)).

Creating one takes a moment: the **Register** link at the top of the page, an
email address, a name and a password.

> Some clubs turn accounts off, and then everyone is a guest. If you cannot see
> a registration link, that is how your club has set it up.

## 11. Email notifications

If you gave an email address, you can be told when:

- **somebody signs up for your game**, or cancels — you know whether you have a
  full table before you even leave the house,
- **something changes about a game you are signed up for** — a different time, a
  different table — or when a game is called off. No need to keep refreshing the
  page just in case,
- **a poll you took part in is decided** — you get told what you are actually
  playing,
- **something new appears at a meetup** you are waiting for — if the club has
  switched that list on, there is a box under the table plan for subscribing.

Every one of those emails has an unsubscribe link at the bottom. One click and
it stops — you do not have to ask anyone.

Notifications are per **meetup**. Subscribing to news about the April game night
does not subscribe you to the May one.

## 12. Your game library

This one is for people with an account: a list of the games you own at home and
could bring along.

What for? So the rest of the club knows what to ask you for. Somebody wants to
play a particular title, checks who owns it, and asks.

You open your library from a button in your user panel. Games are added in three
ways:

- **searching by title** — just like adding a game to a table,
- **pasting a link** to the game on BoardGameGeek,
- **by hand**, if the game is not there.

If you have a BoardGameGeek account and keep your collection there, you can
**fetch the whole thing**: you give your profile address and the site pulls it
all in at once.

> **Fetching a collection takes a moment** — sometimes ten seconds or more. The
> button will show a spinner. Wait calmly and do not click a second time. If you
> get a message saying the collection is still being prepared, wait a minute and
> try again — the second attempt usually works.

For each game you can mark whether you are currently able to bring it. Games you
would rather not show to anyone, simply do not add.

## 13. Comments and messages

Games and polls can carry **comments**, if the club has switched them on. Handy
for things like "I can bring a second copy" or "I will be ten minutes late".

There is also **messaging**: to the person bringing a game, or to everyone
signed up for it. The message goes by email, but you do not see the addresses —
neither theirs, nor they yours.

### Chat

Some clubs also have a **chat** — a small speech-bubble window, usually in the
corner of the screen. You click it, type your name, and write to everyone who
happens to be looking at the page. Good for small things like "I am at the
door" or "anyone fancy something short before 18:00?".

You do not need an account. New messages appear on their own, without
refreshing the page.

> **Cannot see that window?** It means the organiser has not switched chat on
> for your club. Nothing is broken — the feature is simply turned off.

## 14. Common questions

**I signed up and changed my mind.**
Click **Resign** next to your name. The sooner the better for whoever is on the
reserve list.

**I cannot cancel — it is asking for some email address.**
You signed up as a guest, so the site is making sure it is you. Give the same
address you used when signing up. Do not remember it? Ask the organiser — they
can remove anyone.

**I signed up twice / I typed my name wrong.**
Resign and sign up again. If that will not work, the organiser can fix it.

**All the seats are taken and I really want to play.**
Put yourself on the reserve list. People drop out more often than you would
think.

**I added a game to the wrong table.**
If you added it while logged in, or gave an email address, you can edit it or
move it. Otherwise ask the organiser.

**I do not know the rules to any of these games.**
That is a very common position to be in. Sign up for a game where somebody has
said they will teach the rules — that is exactly why the information is there.

**Do I have to create an account?**
No. An account only makes life easier ([section 10](#10-accounts--why-bother)).

**Something is not working.**
Write to the person organising the meetup. Describe what you clicked and what
happened — that is usually enough to find the cause quickly.
